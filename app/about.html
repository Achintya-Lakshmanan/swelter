Standard city opens no model-selected sites. Open cooling/warming sites prioritizes 1, 3 or 5 facilities using predicted risk, proximity, vulnerability and posted opening hours.

The map marks selected sites. Metrics show predicted baseline risk-hours, risk-hours within site reach, risk-hours outside coverage and at-risk synthetic residents within reach. Resident movement shows baseline routines.

Reachable risk is not prevented exposure: attendance, route safety, current facility conditions and capacity are unverified.

Real inputs. Synthetic residents.

City cooling/warming site records and opening hours, NOAA weather and city heat maps provide the physical context. Census ACS, EIA RECS, CDC PLACES and ATUS inform residents and their routines.

Two random forests learn simulated dangerous-exposure labels. They are not trained on observed shelter or health outcomes. Each model uses 79,560 resident-hour examples across three historical weather days. Unhoused residents are oversampled and their schedules are assumed; counts are not Philadelphia population estimates.

The typical winter-day unseen-weather test did not beat a constant-risk baseline. The app discloses this limitation alongside the model's other held-out results.
