Who does the heat reach first?

Swelter replays Philadelphia’s real heatwave and winter-storm days, hour by hour, for 1,560 synthetic residents, and shows which cooling or warming sites reach the people at risk.

Replay Jul 20, 2019or Jan 2, 2018 in the cold

The simulator itself, running: the real heat index at PHL on Jul 20 2019, every dot a synthetic resident following a real time-use diary. Click any dot to see their day.

A real day, not a scenario.

Each replay is a day that happened. Severe heat is Jul 20, 2019: the 12th-worst of 206 hot days at Philadelphia International since 2010. The heat index climbed from 88°F at 6 AM to 107°F at 6 PM.

Four real inputs, one honest question.

First, the weather. NOAA hourly records at PHL for three real days per hazard: typical, severe, record.

Then, the people. 1,560 residents built from Census ACS tracts, RECS air-conditioning rates and CDC PLACES health data.

Then, their routines. Each weekday is a real American Time Use Survey diary, matched by age and work.

Last, the sites. The city’s 31 real cooling and warming sites with posted hours. A trained model ranks which 1, 3 or 5 reach the most risk.

Checked against held-out data.

Scores measure agreement with simulated and observed behaviour, not health outcomes. Where a test failed, the app says so.

0.519 h
Heat model’s held-out daily exposure error per resident (winter: 0.318 h).
0.6 pp
Behaviour fitted to Google mobility 2020–21, off by 0.6 percentage points on held-out 2022 hot days across six categories.
1 fail
The typical winter-day unseen-weather test did not beat a constant-risk baseline. The simulator flags it on that day.

Reachable risk is not prevented exposure: attendance, route safety, capacity and current facility conditions are unverified. Residents are synthetic; counts are not population estimates.
